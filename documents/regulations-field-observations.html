Regulations Governing the Conduct of Field Observations

Five Rules for the Peripatetic Researcher

The following regulations govern the conduct of field observations made under the auspices of the Institute for Peripatetic Studies. They are brief, on the grounds that a researcher who requires extensive rules about how to pay attention in the field has already encountered a difficulty that regulations cannot resolve. They are nonetheless binding.

Regulation I — Record What Was Actually Observed

Field notes shall record what was actually seen, heard, or otherwise directly encountered during the excursion. The temptation to improve an observation — to record what it would have been more interesting to find, or what subsequent reflection suggests ought to have been there — shall be resisted.

The record need not be comprehensive; it is understood that full comprehension of a landscape is not available in a single walk. But the observations that are recorded should be honest ones. The Institute considers an accurate note about something unremarkable to be of considerably more value than an embellished note about something that was only partially observed.

Regulation II — Distinguish Evidence from Inference

Field notes shall distinguish, wherever practicable, between what was directly observed and what was inferred from that observation. The notation observed and inferred need not appear explicitly; but the researcher should be clear, at least privately, about which category applies to any given entry.

An inference made on the basis of a single observation is tentative. An inference that cannot be stated without recourse to assumptions the researcher cannot verify is speculative. The Institute does not discourage speculation, but it requires that speculation be labeled as such.

Regulation III — Acknowledge What Was Not Determined

Where an observation raised a question that the excursion did not resolve, the question shall be recorded. The Institute regards an unresolved question as a legitimate and valuable outcome of a field excursion, not as evidence of incomplete work.

Uncertainty about the significance of an observation — uncertainty about its cause, its history, or its place within the larger character of the landscape — shall be recorded with the same care as the observation itself. The limits of what is known are part of the record.

Regulation IV — Respect the Places and People Encountered

Peripatetic inquiry is conducted in places that belong to, are inhabited by, or are otherwise of significance to people who did not undertake to be studied. The researcher's curiosity does not confer an entitlement to access, attention, or information. Field observations should be conducted with a degree of consideration for this fact.

The Institute does not issue detailed guidance on what this consideration requires in specific circumstances, on the grounds that such guidance cannot adequately substitute for judgment. What it requires, generally, is the willingness to conduct one's inquiry at whatever distance from others is appropriate to the situation.

Regulation V — Document Departures from the Intended Route

Where the excursion took a route other than the one originally planned, the departure shall be noted. The note need record only the fact of departure, the approximate point at which it occurred, and whether it was deliberate, accidental, or a combination of the two. No further explanation is required unless the departure produced observations of particular interest, in which case those observations shall be recorded with appropriate care.

The purpose of this regulation is not to enforce adherence to planned routes — the Institute explicitly does not require this — but to ensure that the actual route taken is recoverable from the record. A field note that cannot be located in a particular part of a particular landscape is a note that cannot be returned to, corrected, or built upon.

Issued under the authority of the Institute for Peripatetic Studies, Collegium Itinerantium. These regulations supplement and are consistent with the academic regulations set out in Article V of the Founding Charter. Anno Domini MMXXIV.
