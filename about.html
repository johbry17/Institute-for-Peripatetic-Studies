Collegium Itinerantium

About the Institute

On wandering, observation, and the serious study of place

The Peripatetic Tradition

The name derives from the Greek peripatein — to walk about. The Peripatetics of antiquity understood that movement and thought are not rivals; they are companions.

The impulse to know the world through direct encounter with it is as old as inquiry itself. Naturalists have always walked their territories. Geographers have always traveled their subjects. Historians have always visited their sites. What has been lacking is a dedicated institutional framework — a home for the conviction that movement is not merely a means of getting to the place of study, but is itself a fundamental mode of knowing.

The Institute for Peripatetic Studies exists to provide that framework. Solvitur Ambulando — it is solved by walking — is not merely our motto. It is our methodology, our epistemology, and our answer to most objections raised from behind a desk.

Founding Story

The Institute traces its formal establishment to an October afternoon in 2024 when the Founding Director set out to locate a particular lane that had appeared on an 1880s insurance map and was not clearly accounted for in the current street record. The lane, when located, proved to be a short, unremarkable passage between a car park and a drainage easement. What proved remarkable was the route taken to find it.

The search had involved three neighborhoods, a demolition site of uncertain vintage, an unexpectedly large park that did not appear under any name on the maps consulted, and a final approach through a residential district considerably more interesting than the intended destination. It was during the return journey — specifically, while resting at a bench positioned to face a stretch of fence and a partially visible roofline with no obvious explanation for its orientation — that the Founding Director recognized the problem.

The problem was this: the walk had produced genuine observations. Several of them were interesting. None of them had been the walk's stated purpose, which had been simply to verify the continued existence of a lane. The existing scholarly frameworks for field research offered no satisfactory account of what had just occurred. The detour, the digression, the accidental discovery of the unnamed park — these were not failures to reach the destination. They were, the Founding Director concluded, the substance of the inquiry. The destination had merely provided an occasion.

A notebook was produced. Several observations were recorded with perhaps more care than the occasion strictly required. On the back page, the outlines of what would become the Institute's founding principles were sketched in the compact, overly formal prose that the Founding Director has since recognized as a sign of intellectual excitement rather than actual clarity. The notebook is retained in the Institute's archive and is periodically consulted to verify that the founding principles remain consistent with what was actually written, which they approximately are.

The Institute was formally constituted in the weeks that followed. The founding charter was drafted, the seal commissioned, and the doctorate conferred. The lane, for the record, has since been verified as extant. It remains unremarkable.

Mission

The Institute for Peripatetic Studies advances Peripatetics as an intellectual tradition: the interdisciplinary study of place through movement, combining geography, ecology, history, culture, observation, cartography, and conversation to understand landscapes as living systems.

We hold that landscapes are not static backdrops to human activity but active, layered, intelligible objects of inquiry. We hold that encountering a place on foot — attending to its textures, its boundaries, its histories, its inhabitants — produces knowledge that cannot be extracted from maps, databases, or aerial photographs alone, however excellent those resources may be.

The Institute's mission is to advance these forms of inquiry, to formalize their methods, and to confer upon their practitioners the institutional recognition that they have long merited.

Philosophical Principles

The Institute's intellectual commitments are organized around four propositions.

Movement changes perspective. The walker encounters the world at a pace that permits sustained attention. She moves through transitions rather than jumping between snapshots. She experiences scale, effort, and weather as genuine data. To walk through a landscape is to receive an argument about it that no other medium can deliver in quite the same form.

Direct observation matters. The Institute maintains a healthy skepticism toward conclusions reached exclusively through secondary sources. This does not make it anti-scholarly; it makes it insistent that the scholarly record and the observable world be kept in productive conversation. The field note and the monograph belong together.

Curiosity is interdisciplinary by nature. Landscapes do not organize themselves according to academic departments. A riverbank is simultaneously ecological, historical, geological, cultural, and aesthetic. The Institute encourages its researchers to follow questions wherever they lead, across departmental boundaries and across terrain.

Not every journey requires optimization. The Institute is explicitly skeptical of the assumption that purposeful movement must be efficient, goal-directed, and justifiable by its outputs. The detour, the digression, and the unhurried return by a longer route are legitimate scholarly activities.

Not every journey requires a destination.
Not every question requires a desk.
Not every excursion requires justification.

Research Methods

The Institute recognizes the following as legitimate and indispensable modes of peripatetic inquiry:

  • Walking
  • Observation
  • Conversation
  • Mapping
  • Sketching
  • Photography
  • Field Notes
  • Data Analysis
  • Historical Research

These methods are complementary, not competing. The best peripatetic inquiry draws from several simultaneously, moving between the observational and the analytical as the work demands. Solvitur ambulando applies to methodological problems as much as to any other kind.

The Institute's ongoing record of observations, inquiries, and proposed studies is collected in the Field Notes archive.
